## What M1 demonstrably struggles with

1. **Crop-inside center selection followed by feedback drift is the clearest system-level bottleneck.** In archived M1 predictions, 365/377 sustained severe-overlap failure runs begin with valid GT fully inside the reconstructed search crop; 220 later leave the crop, with a median 19.5-frame onset-to-exit delay. In 27,674 crop-inside severe frames, 79.8% have center error greater than half the GT diagonal, whereas 45.2% have such a center error while predicted area remains within 0.5-2 times GT. These are output/geometry descriptions, not a proof that the CENTER head is the first faulty operator. Archived boxes are integer-valued, so reconstructed crops approximate the true online state.
2. **Risk concentrates in difficult scene groups.** Among 244 test sequences, 62 have AUC below 40 and account for 62.17% of valid severe-mismatch frames. The smallest-target quartile averages AUC 43.35 versus 65.63 for the largest; the highest relative-motion quartile averages 48.57. The smallest-target/highest-motion intersection (25 sequences) averages 38.28. TC (thermal crossover), SA (similar appearance), LR and LI tags are enriched among low-AUC sequences but are overlapping sequence-level labels, not frame-level proof of modality pollution or a gate target. Large-object failures also exist.
3. **The fusion operator is not shown to be the first point of loss.** In 16 train-side mismatch event sequences, a sequence-held-out linear probe found pre-fusion RGB/TIR-concatenated target-background margins of -15.913 (pre-onset) and -18.220 (severe onset); post-fusion margins were less negative, -4.377 and -6.038. Thus a systematic extra collapse after `tbsi_fuse_search` is unsupported. The probe measures decodability, not what the trained CENTER head uses. Current-worktree replay differs from the protected archive at 82,219/219,048 frames by more than 2 px; this result cannot pinpoint the original archived M1's first bad layer, nor prove that MHSA itself discarded target information.
4. **A fragile fusion-to-CENTER peak interface exists in some fixed states.** In `bikeboystrong` frame 103 at the LCGA candidate's exact prior state, the same M1 fusion/head weights give center error 1.30 px without the trained LCGA residual and 33.10 px with it; the response margin shifts from a near-tie (+0.00029) to -0.00559. Another loss (`openningumbrella`) is instead explained by earlier state drift: both arms fail on the candidate's true state. These are post-hoc cases from already-viewed train-side dev sequences, not a prevalence estimate or a reason to tune on those frames.

## What the module trials do and do not identify

| Trial | Direct observation | Diagnostic implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official LCGA, matched joint continuation | AUC -1.0643 on its frozen dev30; center error improved in 15/30; FPS -17.46%. | This pre-fusion two-stream local operator did not pass the localization screen under joint training. Upstream training drift can dominate individual failures; curvature's immediate contribution was weak in a narrow old-dev intervention. |
| Official LCGA, M1 frozen and only refiner trained | AUC -0.2984 on a different dev30; center error improved in 10/30; FPS -19.99%. | Freezing M1 removes one simple co-adaptation explanation but does not reveal broad local-refinement benefit. Different dev sets must not be numerically compared as a matched pair. |
| Task-adapted Strip-inspired score refiner, M1 frozen | On another untouched train-side short dev30: AUC +0.0045; 1 win/1 loss/28 ties; center error improved in 1/30; 227/9,906 integer boxes changed in only 2 sequences; FPS -0.68%. Original 267 M1 state tensors remain bitwise equal. | The branch updates and sometimes changes predictions, yet it fails the preregistered localization screen. Integer-box equality does not imply the floating-point score maps are equal; no fixed-state score-map utilization distribution was measured after training. |

The evidence does **not** distinguish a universally unsuitable Strip operator from an unsuitable task adaptation. For this *specific* Strip trial, the adaptation/optimization contract is the sharper actionable concern: `StripScoreRefiner` acts only after `CenterPredictor.conv1_ctr` on a 16x16 score branch; size and offset branches are unchanged. `CenterPredictor.cal_bbox` selects a cell with hard `torch.max(...).indices`. Consequently the box L1/GIoU terms do not supply a useful gradient through peak selection to this score-only refiner; its ordinary training signal is the Gaussian heatmap focal loss. That loss can shape scores without explicitly rewarding the GT cell beating the strongest distractor at a fragile onset or improving closed-loop recovery. This is a computational-graph fact, **not** proof that the focal loss caused the observed failure. The learned outer alpha (-0.013339) shows updating, not effective residual strength or peak correction. Three epochs, frozen M1/head, short dev sequences and one seed also limit conclusions.

## Decision and next single-variable test

**Stop the present score-only Strip version. Do not interpret this as "find a stronger paper module".** The main unresolved issue is whether hard onset frames contain enough target evidence in the existing fused feature to let a different score readout win. First do a training-free, fixed-state diagnostic on predeclared *train-side* crop-inside onset/precursor and matched normal frames not chosen from viewed dev outcomes. Save float score maps for fixed M1 and trained Strip on identical template/state/crop. Record GT-cell versus strongest non-target peak margin, top-1/top-2 gap, peak index/GT distance, Strip score-map delta and effective feature residual ratio; stratify by small target/high relative motion and available TC/SA labels without interpreting those labels causally. Include the same-state M1 head score and a held-out supervised linear readout of the fused feature. This distinguishes (a) readable target evidence with a wrong existing peak, (b) score perturbation that is present but cannot flip peaks, and (c) fused evidence already too weak. Do not use test-244 or the already-inspected LCGA/Strip dev sets to choose a design.

If (a) recurs across independent train-side sequences, the **one-change** follow-up is to keep the same lightweight Strip operator, insertion point, initialization, frozen M1 and training data contract, but add an explicit hard-negative peak-ranking term to its existing heatmap objective. Compare it to a freshly rerun score-only control under the same seed/steps and a newly frozen unused train-side dev manifest. Require a negative per-sequence median center-error delta, broad center-error improvement, improved onset GT-vs-distractor margin/peak distance and no gain dominated by one sequence. This tests *task supervision*, not a new operator; keep score ranking bounded so normal-frame peaks are not destabilized. If (b) predominates, first report score-map/residual scale and gradient flow before changing alpha or kernels. If (c) predominates, stop head-side Strip work and investigate target-conditioned search representation or resolution/state recovery upstream; a generic score gate cannot create missing target evidence. A post-fusion local module and RGB/TIR reliability gate should not be promoted without their own interface-level evidence.
