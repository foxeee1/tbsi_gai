`bikeboystrong` at the true candidate state retains similar size predictions at the GT-center cell in the two arms (M1 `[0.203,0.365]`, LCGA `[0.204,0.361]` as crop fractions); the tracked error mainly comes from choosing a different response peak, not a conspicuous size-map collapse. These are not perfect size estimates, and the GT-center cell need not be the head's preferred target-support cell. Template-search cosine margins are negative for both modalities in both arms on this frame; they do not demonstrate that the fused representation unequivocally points to the target. The M1 response margin of +0.00029 is itself nearly a tie. Thus the fixed-state evidence identifies a **peak-selection sensitivity at the fusion-to-CENTER interface**, not a proven isolated head defect. `openningumbrella` also shows that a post-fusion module cannot be assumed to repair upstream state drift.

## Official Strip-R-CNN source audit

Pinned official repository `HVision-NKU/Strip-R-CNN` at commit `c2d3cf4f9c561f4dea6d6779bcc7aaf3c5ae1b78` (local snapshot `output/experiments/M1_LCGA_STRIP_INTERFACE_DIAG_20261010/official_strip_rcnn`). The pasted `StripConv` / `StripModule` matches the **backbone** `StripBlock` / `Attention` structure in `mmrotate/models/backbones/stripnet.py`: depthwise 5x5, depthwise orthogonal strips, 1x1 channel projection, multiplicative gate, then an internal residual. The paper's detection ROI regression head instead uses the smaller `StripBlock` in `mmrotate/models/roi_heads/bbox_heads/reg_block.py`, inserted after a branch conv in `strip_head.py`; it is not the pasted full `StripModule`, and it does not establish efficacy for CENTER score selection. Backbone defaults `k1=1,k2=19`; on TBSI's 16x16 head map, a 19-wide strip extends beyond the entire grid and relies substantially on padding. Directly copying the pasted module at 768 channels also adds two dense 1x1 projections and is not an identity insertion merely because it contains an internal residual.

## Decision

**Conditional go for one training-free, task-adapted Strip-inspired gate; no training yet.** The `bikeboystrong` event supplies a concrete reason to test local spatial recalibration near CENTER, and `whiteboy\`head` shows the peak can move beneficially. The two losses do not justify dropping in the 768-channel pasted module unchanged: one is state-drift driven, and the other is a low-margin response tie whose upstream information status remains ambiguous. If proceeding, use a **single** low-cost insertion in the CENTER score branch after its first 768-to-256 projection, not two RGB/TIR copies and not a full 768-channel pre-head module. Use a grid-sized fixed strip kernel smaller than 16, an explicit outer zero gate for exact M1 parity, and keep M1 fusion and size/offset branches unchanged in the first version. Label this a task-adapted Strip-inspired module, not a faithful Strip-R-CNN head reproduction. First measure zero-gate parity, gradient path, tracker FPS in both orders and GPU memory; stop if the speed cost is unjustified. Any later short-training comparison needs a **new** unused train-side dev manifest and independent experiment identity. Do not select kernel/mode using these already-inspected sequences.
